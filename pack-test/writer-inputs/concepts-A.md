# Twelve ideas to turn into concrete events (set A, two events each = 24 items)

Context for the writer: these ideas come from software that lets people share and keep records on their own devices. For each idea, write TWO different concrete events that illustrate it, with actors and objects, in everyday words.

1. permission-rule: A statement saying who is allowed to do what within a given part of a shared space.
2. grant-or-pass-on: Giving a specific person or device a permission, or passing on a permission you hold to someone else.
3. take-back: Taking a permission back so it can no longer be used.
4. whose-rules: Stating whose rules and which secret keys you are using when you ask a question, so the answer can be compared with someone else's answer.
5. disagreement: Noticing that two people's rules for the same thing contradict each other, and writing down that they disagree.
6. resolve-disagreement: Ending a recorded disagreement, either by combining the competing versions into one or by changing the rule.
7. remove-content: The owner removes the content of something they own so that nobody can read it any more.
8. leave-a-marker: Leaving a marker where something was removed, saying that it existed and was deleted.
9. offer-and-confirm: Offering someone a file, and the recipient confirming that they received it and that it was intact.
10. same-fingerprint: Two copies of a record comparing a short fingerprint of what each computed, to confirm they reached the same conclusion.
11. locked-box: Holding encrypted data you cannot read, and reporting it as "not read" instead of as "empty".
12. ask-a-peer: Asking another device for part of its data, with a limit on how much the request may cost.
